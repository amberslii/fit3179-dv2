Sources & scope

ABS 2021 Census TableBuilder: employment, income and education. Employed people aged 15 and over, by main place of work, across 88 mapped Statistical Areas Level 4 (SA4s). The numerator is employment in the selected industries from Chapter 2. The denominator is all employment in that area, not the total of the four groups.

Food: bakery, confectionery, ice cream, cheese and other dairy. Home: wooden and metal furniture, mattresses and whiteware; tableware is excluded. Care: cleaning compounds, cosmetics and toiletries. Clothing: knitted products and clothing; knitting includes fabric. These are related industries; their jobs cannot all be attributed to the specific products in Chapter 1.

Counts sum 16 mutually exclusive INDP industry-code cells. ABS perturbs published counts for confidentiality: small counts and small differences need care, and a published zero does not prove there are no jobs. Special records such as “no fixed place of work” are not mapped. These 2021 figures are not the regional breakdown of Chapter 2's 2025 figures. Boundaries: ABS ASGS 2021 SA4, simplified for display.

4.1

Does Australian-made influence our choices?

Three separate consumer survey questions · 2026

100 squares = 100% of respondents

Interest, willingness and a reported purchase switch are different measures.

Roy Morgan Research · 2026
Sources & scope

These are independent published survey percentages, not stages of a funnel or observed sales. The same person may contribute to several measures; the percentages must not be added. The 34% figure is self-reported behaviour.

The report describes more than 1,000 consumers; exact sample size, per-question bases and full questionnaire wording are not published. Labels paraphrase the findings.

4.2

What matters when buying Australian-made?

Attributes rated important by consumers · 2026

BEHIND THE CHOICE

Local jobs matter alongside value and quality.

More than 90% rate each of these six factors important. Supporting local jobs matters to 94% of respondents, alongside value and quality at 95%.

These are shoppers’ priorities, not ratings of how Australian-made products perform.

Roy Morgan Research · 2026
Sources & scope

Percentages describe what respondents consider important when buying Australian-made products. They do not measure product quality or performance. Each attribute is rated independently; these are not shares of a total.

All six published values are above 90%. The axes follow value, product attributes, and support for jobs and Australia; their connecting line shows one survey profile, not a time sequence. The filled point highlights local jobs. Exact item bases and uncertainty estimates are not published, so small differences should not be treated as a clear ranking.

2026 research, p. 3 ↗ · Chart data · Vega specification

2023 report · Stated willingness, not actual spending
Sources & scope

The eight published percentages total 99.4%. They are retained as published, without normalisation or an invented missing category. The infographic does not explain the shortfall. Categories stay in premium order; “not willing” and “not sure” are separate responses.

The A$100 example converts the selected premium band into an illustrative price. It is not a product price or an average premium. “Up to 5% more” and “over 50% more” retain the source's boundaries; no unreported lower or upper endpoint is invented.

2023 is the report label; fieldwork dates, exact sample size, respondent age coverage, exact item bases and the full questionnaire are not supplied. These figures are not a trend comparison with the 2026 questions above. The 2026 report does not provide a complete set of clearly exclusive premium bands.
